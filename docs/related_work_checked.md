# Related work: singer identity and production mismatch

## Manuscript text

Singer identification in music is complicated by accompaniment and recording context. Sharma et al. examined source separation as a way to reduce accompaniment interference before singer identification, while Hsieh et al. investigated accompaniment confounding and trained classifiers with separated and remixed vocals and instrumentals [1, 2]. Hsieh et al. evaluated a 20-class classifier on Artist20 with an album split. These studies establish the importance of non-vocal context, but do not directly evaluate the controlled production-mismatch comparisons used in the present work.

Recent representation-learning methods support comparisons of singing voices without restricting evaluation to a fixed singer classification task. Torres et al. trained self-supervised singer-identity encoders and evaluated singer similarity and identification across datasets [3]. Garoufis et al. introduced contrastive vocal similarity modeling (CVSM), including artist-informed and label-agnostic variants; their evaluation includes objective tasks and a listener study of model-retrieved vocal similarity [4]. The present study examines selected frozen encoders from these families under mismatched production conditions, alongside speech and general music encoders. Its listener task is intended to compare singer-identity decisions under those conditions; it should not be presented as the first listener study of vocal similarity.

Sensitivity to recording transformations is also relevant beyond singer identity. Deng et al. studied how parameterized gain, filtering, reverberation, and bitcrushing change general audio embeddings, and assessed implications for instrument classification [5]. AudioCodecBench evaluates codecs on several downstream tasks, including singer identification on VocalSet [6]. These studies motivate the question of whether audio processing affects representations, but their reported tasks differ from a controlled singer-verification comparison of clean queries, processed same-singer candidates, and clean impostors. Balakrishnan and Thayasivam address singer identification in degraded archival recordings with quality-aware model selection and accompaniment disentanglement [7]; their work is especially relevant to claims about robustness and real-world degradation.

The present study focuses on the combined setting of production mismatch in singer-similarity comparisons, including matched-processing controls and comparisons across encoder types. A reduction in error when both candidates are processed similarly is consistent with an effect of relative processing conditions; it does not by itself prove that processing preserved all identity information or establish the model's internal mechanism. Claims regarding real rooms, real songs, and human judgments should be limited to their respective evaluated conditions and documented results.
